2. Problems found and fixed

Five problems came up. The first three are fixed and tested. The last two are fixed in the code but not yet tested.

2.1 RYU's event loop crashed at startup

SymptomThe 22:07 session ran for 36 minutes with no switches registered ("switches": {}), a single START event, and 526 dead connections on port 6653.
CauseThe orchestrator stored its event history in self.events, which overwrote the event queue of the same name in RYU's base class. Log: 'collections.deque' object has no attribute 'get'.
FixRenamed the attribute to self.event_log. The /v2x/events URL is unchanged.
StatusTested. All 3 switches connect, and the crash no longer appears.

2.2 Vehicle-to-vehicle pings failed across RSUs after moving

Symptompingall reached only 74, 74 and 81 of 90. Every failure was vehicle-to-vehicle between RSUs, and all vehicle-to-server pings worked.
CauseThe old RSU keeps a departed vehicle in its station list for minutes (one entry was still there about 7.5 minutes in). The best explanation is that its kernel delivers frames for that vehicle locally over Wi-Fi, so they never reach OVS. This was inferred from station lists, not from frame captures.
EvidenceStation dumps matched the failures exactly. At the end of the diagnostic run, the only stale entry left was veh2 on rsu1, and the only failures left were veh2 with veh3, veh4 and veh5.
FixAdded the hostapd settings used in the Saraiva et al. code (ap_max_inactivity=6, skip_inactivity_poll=1, max_listen_interval=6) plus 1-second vehicle beacons. The beacons also let RYU see vehicles during movement.
StatusTested. pingall reached 89, 90 and 90 of 90, and 4 HANDOVER events appeared where there had been none.

2.3 Same-RSU traffic bypassed its slice meter

SymptomA 1 Mb/s platoon flow between two vehicles on one RSU delivered 260 of 260 packets, yet the platoon rule counters stayed at 0 on every switch.
CauseThe RSU's kernel delivered the frames itself, so they never reached OVS. The meter could not see or limit them.
FixSet client_isolation=True in the topology. The controller now finds each RSU's Wi-Fi port and sends same-RSU traffic back out through it using OFPP_IN_PORT, including broadcasts such as ARP. The STALE check now applies to wired ports only.
StatusTested. 229 packets counted on rsu1 and 0 elsewhere, as expected. An 8 Mb/s flood was limited to 3.96 Mb/s same-RSU and 3.92 Mb/s cross-RSU. pingall reached 90/90 in all three runs.

2.4 Traffic gaps of 6 to 14 seconds during handover

SymptomIn the first pilot, flows stopped for 6 to 14 seconds while vehicles moved, with loss of 24% to 75%. The longest gaps were nearly identical in the static and orchestrated runs and similar under best effort.
Likely causeMininet-WiFi disconnects a vehicle once it is out of range. The gaps suggest a coverage hole between the two RSUs, which are 40 m apart, so this would not be a slicing effect. The actual range has not been measured.
FixAdded RSU_RANGE = 30, which should give 20 m of overlap.
StatusNot tested. Setting range may not change the simulated signal strength. If it does not, vehicles at the edge could lose packets or fail to associate.

2.5 The heavy scenario was too light to test the loop

SymptomThe backhaul never overflowed (about 18.5 Mb/s of 20 Mb/s under static slicing). PROTECT_OFF also had no time to fire before the trial ended.
FixRaised critical traffic to about 8.5 Mb/s on the wire (URLLC-far 570 packets/s, platoon 450 packets/s, 1000 bytes each). With eMBB at up to 12 Mb/s, the total can exceed 20 Mb/s. Added 5 seconds at the end of each parked trial.
StatusNot tested. This load was chosen so the loop has a case where it could matter. The write-up should say so and report the result even if it shows no benefit.

5. How the literature survey was used

The survey PDF itself does not cover the technical bugs. The most useful source was the code repository it links for Saraiva et al. (github.com/saraivacode/framework_its_sdn), which supplied the hostapd settings.

SourceWhat it shaped
Saraiva et al., IEEE T-ITS 2021 (paper 7)RSU hostapd settings and the idea of beacon traffic. The three-way comparison: best effort, QoS only and framework. The metrics PDR, throughput and RTT.
Shah et al., IEEE TCE 2025 (paper 2)Measuring safety traffic during handover, not only while vehicles are parked.
Ahmad, Gul & Derawi, Sensors 2026 (paper 1)Supports four slices and the focus on infotainment congesting critical traffic.
Survey's combined research gapThe threshold loop, benchmarked on latency, jitter, throughput and loss. This is why the static setup exists: only that comparison shows what the loop adds.


Limits: the Saraiva settings came from reading its code, not the paper's full text, so check the paper before citing them as its method. The beacon script itself (carcont2.sh) was not read, so the 1-second ping beacon is this project's own version. The survey says papers 3, 4 and 5 were not read in full, so they were not relied on here.

Parked timeline: 25 s settling, then 15 s idle, 30 s flood and 15 s recovery. Results skip the first 5 s of the flood.
The handover scenario runs while vehicles move, for 30 s: URLLC far (veh1 to mec_rsu1), URLLC edge (veh3 to mec_rsu1), platoon (veh2 to veh4, 1000 B at 100/s) and the eMBB flood. In that scenario the "edge" and "far" labels describe where each vehicle ends up.

Metrics

For each flow: RTT (mean, 95th and 99th percentile), jitter, loss (PDR is 100% minus loss), throughput and the longest delivery gap. The controller log adds PROTECT_ON, PROTECT_OFF and HANDOVER events. Replies carry only a 12-byte header, because both directions share one meter and full-size replies would use up the budget twice.

What the pilot suggests

  • Slicing protects critical traffic. Under both slicing setups, critical RTT stayed close to idle, and critical loss was 0%.
  • Most of the benefit comes from the static meters. In congestion, the loop improved mean RTT by under 1 ms and halved eMBB throughput. In the old heavy load, it improved mean platoon RTT by about 2 ms and 99th percentile by about 10 ms, again at half the eMBB throughput.
  • The eMBB limit works. eMBB throughput stayed under its limit in the parked runs: 11.3 of 12 Mb/s under static, and 5.6 of 6 Mb/s under orchestrated.
  • Wi-Fi is sometimes a bottleneck too. URLLC edge does not cross the backhaul, yet it reached 688 ms with 17.3 Mb/s of eMBB. The best explanation is that the RSU's Wi-Fi queue also saturates. In the heavy best-effort run at 12.1 Mb/s it stayed at 1.7 ms. So best-effort delay is not purely a backhaul effect.
  • Do not use the first pilot's handover results. They reflect the suspected coverage hole.

8. What needs to be done next

Step 1: Run the second pilot (about 8 minutes)

sudo bash ~/v2x-project/scripts/run_step2_host.sh 1 "heavy handover"

Check that:

  • Handover gaps fall well below the 6 to 14 seconds seen so far, and vehicles still change RSU (4 HANDOVER events).
  • heavy under static now shows backhaul pressure, and PROTECT_OFF fires before each trial ends.

If the gaps remain or vehicles stop associating, range did not behave as expected. In that case, try the propagation exponent next (currently exp=5; the Saraiva code uses 2.8), and measure the real coverage distance first.

Step 2: Rerun the Step 12 check

The topology has changed since Step 12 passed, so connectivity needs confirming. This also re-exercises the meter and handover checks. It needs the plot display enabled first.

DISPLAY=:1 xhost +local:docker
DISPLAY=:1 sudo --preserve-env=DISPLAY bash ~/v2x-project/scripts/verify_step12_host.sh

Expect 90/90 after warm-up, 4 HANDOVER events, and same-RSU platoon counters above 0. Note that the script overwrites logs/step12, so earlier runs are kept in the numbered folders.

Step 3: Decide the threshold policy

This is the main open decision. The loop currently halves eMBB whenever critical traffic reaches 500 kb/s on a switch, even if 12 Mb/s of eMBB would still fit. The options are:

OptionForAgainst
Keep the current fixed cutSimple, already tested, and easy to explainGives up half of eMBB throughput, possibly for little benefit
Capacity-aware cut (eMBB budget = link capacity minus critical load minus a margin)Only cuts eMBB when needed, which may make a stronger contributionNew code and more tuning. All setups need rerunning afterwards


Base this on the new heavy result. If the fixed cut shows a clear benefit when the backhaul overflows, keeping it and explaining the trade-off is reasonable. If not, the capacity-aware cut is worth trying. Decide this before the full repeats, because changing it later would mean rerunning them.

Step 4: Finish the remaining functional tests

  • REST PUT resize, confirming the meter changes on the switches and that traffic follows.
  • URLLC, mMTC and best-effort meter limits under an overload. So far only platoon has been directly tested with a flood, and eMBB has only been seen staying under its limit.

Step 5: Run the full experiments (about 70 minutes for 5 repeats)

Keep the pilot logs first, because the runner overwrites matching trial folders:

mv ~/v2x-project/logs/step2 ~/v2x-project/logs/step2_pilot2
sudo bash ~/v2x-project/scripts/run_step2_host.sh 5
python3 ~/v2x-project/experiments/analyze.py

Stop other containers and heavy programs on the laptop during the run. Five repeats is a practical starting point, not a calculated sample size, so raise it if the spread between repeats is large.

Step 6: Analyse and write up

  • Plot RTT distributions and throughput by setup, with error bars across repeats.
  • State the limitations: beacons on the best-effort slice, the chosen heavy load, Wi-Fi as a second bottleneck, no nearest-MEC steering after handover, and scripted mobility in place of SUMO.
  • Pull the full text of the Saraiva paper before citing it, and of papers 3, 4 and 5 before using them, as the survey itself advises.

Housekeeping

  • Initialise a git repository, so that the exact code behind each result can be identified.
